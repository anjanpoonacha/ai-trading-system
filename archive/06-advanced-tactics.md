# Advanced Tactics

## Pyramiding

### Concept
- Buy MORE of a winning stock at HIGHER price (opposite of averaging down)
- Goal: size big on strongest stocks that prove themselves
- Only for experienced traders — beginners should avoid

### Requirements to Pyramid
1. Stock must be **exceptionally strong** (super strong structure)
2. Must have **super strong confirmation** before adding (wait for price to prove itself)
3. Must either raise RPT, raise stop loss, or both
4. Pyramid opportunity must look like **a valid setup in itself**

### Rules
- **Do NOT pyramid out of the base** — only within the base or near breakout level
- Do NOT pyramid at random locations during up swings
- Do NOT pyramid on weak/insufficient confirmation
- Can pyramid even after partial exit, as long as stock is within base
- Gap ups at right locations = great pyramid confirmation
- Wait for close for 100% confirmation before pyramiding

### Three Approaches (Stop Loss)
| Approach | Action | Position Size Impact |
|----------|--------|---------------------|
| Conservative | Keep SL same | Moderate increase |
| Moderate | Raise SL | Larger increase |
| Aggressive | Raise SL significantly | Maximum increase |

### Three Approaches (RPT)
| Approach | Action | Risk Impact |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| Conservative | Make RPT = 0 (risk-free pyramid) | No additional risk |
| Moderate | Keep RPT same (e.g., 0.5%) | Same risk maintained |
| Aggressive | Raise RPT (e.g., 0.5% → 1%) | Increased risk |

### When to Use Which
- **Pyramiding near entry price** → can only raise RPT (no room to raise SL)
- **Pyramiding at higher price with clear swing low** → can raise SL + adjust RPT
- First buy: standard RPT (e.g., 0.5%). Pyramid: raise to 0.8–1% if confident

### Mathematical Impact
- Without pyramid: 4.4% account change on winner
- Moderate pyramid (same RPT): 6% account change
- Aggressive pyramid (raised RPT): 8.8% account change
- On extended winners: difference becomes exponential

### Pyramid Calculator
- Input: account value, entry price, SL, quantity, new entry price, revised SL, revised risk%
- Output: exact quantity to buy for pyramid
- Available in trading journal (auto-calculates from position data)

## Margin Trading / Leverage

### Why Leverage is Needed
- In strong bull markets: trades become risk-free (SL at cost) → TO% drops to 0
- Exposure reaches 100% but risk is near zero → need leverage to take new setups
- Leverage allows exposure up to 4× (400%)

### Three Methods (India)
1. **Futures** — automatic 4–5× leverage, lower STT cost, 1–1.5L margin for ₹7–8L position
2. **MTF (Margin Trading Facility)** — broker funds 70%, you fund 30%; interest ~0.045%/day
3. **Pledging** — pledge existing holdings as collateral; 50/50 rule (max 50% from collateral without interest); instant with Dhan broker

### Rules
- Only use leverage in **strong/very strong markets** when positions are risk-free
- Max ~20–25 open positions even with leverage
- Do not take trades with very low RPT just to fill positions

## Live Entries (15-Minute Time Frame)

### Concept
- Switch from 50% live + 50% close → **100% live entry** using 15-min chart
- Use 15-min to identify structures, breakout points, and tighter stop losses
- Tighter SL → larger position size → maximized gains

### Benefits
- Lower cost price → better risk-reward
- Tighter stops → bigger position
- Better timing using intraday structure

### Requirements
- Sufficient experience identifying good entry locations
- Quick decision-making ability
- Alert throughout market hours (set alerts, act when triggered)
- More effort in shortlist preparation and chart monitoring

### Execution
- Identify structure on 15-min chart (resistance/support areas)
- Enter on breakout of 15-min structure
- Place SL at swing low on 15-min (must be tighter than TRP, never wider)
- First entry: don't over-tighten SL — use level near TRP for room

## Discretion

### Definition
- Thoughtful adjustments with **full awareness** — NOT breaking rules recklessly
- Arises from intuition built through long-term dedication and system-following
- Only for experienced traders with deep market understanding

### What is NOT Discretion
- Holding past stop loss because "I feel it will come back"
- Lowering stop loss as stock approaches it
- Going aggressive after losses to "recover"
- Buying a stock without a setup because "it looks strong"

### Discretionary Entries (Lower Time Frame)
- Stock doesn't show clean contraction on daily, but 15-min shows:
  - Huge volume expansion + clear resistance area + breakout with volume = valid entry
- Pre-breakout entry: 15-min forms congestion/contraction below daily trigger → enter on green candle confirmation before daily breakout
- Can do 50/50: some at pre-breakout, some at actual breakout

### Discretionary Position Sizing
- Double RPT on **ultimate setups** (picture-perfect, zero doubt)
- Go aggressive when **cluster of stocks from same sector** all set up simultaneously
- 5 trades/quarter at 2× RPT with 60–70% WR → returns jump from 57% to 70–90%

### Discretionary Stop Losses
- **Standard**: entry − TRP
- **Conservative** (broad): trigger level − TRP
- **Aggressive** (tight): low of entry candle, or 15-min swing low
- **Combination**: 50% at aggressive SL, 50% at standard SL

### Discretionary Exits
- Extreme extension + experience says "this is the top" → exit 100% at close (skip midpoint trail rule)
- Weak market pilot trade: exit at cost when stock returns to entry (don't wait for SL)
- Small position in weak market: exit 100% on first partial opportunity (not meaningful to hold)
- Strong stock with more upside: exit only 20% on extension instead of 40%, hold remaining 20% with tight trail
- Broad SL + stock at cost: can hold for SL. Tight SL + stock at cost: exit at cost immediately
