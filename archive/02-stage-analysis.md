# Stage Analysis

## What are Stages
Stocks cycle through 4 stages: Accumulation → Growth → Distribution → Decline. The cycle repeats. Understanding the current stage determines whether to buy, hold, or avoid a stock.

## Stage 1 — Accumulation
- **Characteristics:**
  - Price moves sideways in a range for months/years
  - No sustained demand or supply — trendless
  - Market participants broadly uninterested
  - Big players slowly buying (accumulating) based on future information
  - Rounding bottom structures common
  - Volume spikes appear (institutional footprints) despite no price movement
- **What to do:** Watch only. Do not buy. Wait for breakout confirmation.

## Stage 2 — Growth
- **Characteristics:**
  - Higher highs and higher lows (uptrend)
  - Price above 200 DMA
  - 200 DMA sloping up (for at least 1 month)
  - 50 DMA above 200 DMA
  - In strong Stage 2: price sustains above 50 DMA for most of the move
  - Volume transition: average volumes rise vs accumulation period
  - Up swings on high volume, pullbacks on low volume
  - Frequent positive pivotal candles (large green candles with volume)
  - Small bases form and break out repeatedly
  - Negative pivotal candles are rare
  - Positive news/results come AFTER the move has started
  - Can last 1–3 years
- **What to do:** This is where we buy. Trade in direction of trend. Stage 2 can start directly from Stage 4 (no clear Stage 1).

## Stage 3 — Distribution
- **Characteristics:**
  - Price moves sideways at top after growth stage
  - Stock unable to make new highs — trend exhaustion
  - Rounding top structures (negative sign)
  - Big players selling to retail investors
  - Retail buying maintains demand while institutions create supply
  - Stock unable to maintain uptrend
  - Intensity of moves slows down
  - Stock spends months at same level (range-bound at top)
- **What to do:** Exit positions. Do not buy. Prepare for decline.

## Stage 4 — Decline
- **Characteristics:**
  - Lower lows and lower highs (downtrend)
  - Price below 200 DMA
  - 200 DMA sloping down
  - 50 DMA below 200 DMA
  - Supports break repeatedly
  - Negative pivotal candles with big volume
  - May see positive pivotal candles too (unlike Stage 2 which rarely has negative ones)
  - Good news/earnings may still appear even as price falls — trust price over information
  - Retail gets trapped averaging down
- **What to do:** Never buy. Charts reveal information before news. Trust price over fundamentals.

## How to Identify Current Stage

### Stage 2 Confirmation (all required):
1. Price > 200 DMA
2. 200 DMA sloping up (≥ 1 month)
3. 50 DMA > 200 DMA
4. Higher highs and higher lows structure

### Stage 2 Strength Signals (supporting):
- Price sustains above 50 DMA for most of the move
- Volume transition (rising average volumes)
- Frequent positive pivotal candles
- Bases are small/shallow and break out cleanly
- Up moves on high volume, pullbacks on low volume

### Stage 2 Violation Signals:
- Large negative pivotal candle with high volume
- Price breaks below 200 DMA
- Support breakdown
- 50 DMA crosses below 200 DMA
- Rounding top forming
- Bases become very large/deep
- Breakouts fail repeatedly

### Stage 4 Confirmation:
1. Price < 200 DMA
2. 200 DMA sloping down
3. 50 DMA < 200 DMA
4. Lower highs and lower lows
5. Supports breaking repeatedly

## Entry/Exit Rules Based on Stages

### Entry (Buy):
- Only buy stocks in confirmed Stage 2
- Ideal entry: breakout from accumulation structure (Stage 1 → Stage 2 transition)
- Also valid: base breakout within an existing Stage 2
- Breakout must come with strong volume and positive pivotal candle
- Mini base breakout (minor resistance break) can signal early Stage 2 start

### Exit (Sell):
- When Stage 2 is violated: negative pivotal candle + price below 200 DMA + support breakdown
- When distribution characteristics appear (range-bound at top, trend exhaustion)
- When 50 DMA crosses below 200 DMA
- Never hold into confirmed Stage 4

### Never Buy:
- Stocks in Stage 4 (decline) — regardless of "cheap" price
- Stocks in unclear stages (range-bound, not confirming Stage 2)
- Stocks where breakout fails and structure deteriorates

## Market Context (Index Analysis)
- Use Nifty 500 (preferred over Nifty 50) for broad market stage
- Nifty 500 in Stage 2 = bull market = many stocks in growth stage
- Nifty 500 in Stage 4 = bear market = few stocks in Stage 2
- Stocks move in groups — check sectoral indexes for sector strength
- Stage 2 stocks often show sectoral strength (multiple stocks in same sector rising)
