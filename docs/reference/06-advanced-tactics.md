# Advanced Tactics Reference

## Pyramiding

### Definition
Buying more shares of the same stock at a **higher price** than the previous cost price (opposite of averaging down).

### Core Principle
- **Losing traders**: Size big in weaker stocks (averaging down)
- **Winning traders**: Size big in stronger stocks (pyramiding up)

### Prerequisites for Pyramiding
1. **Stock must be super strong** — exceptional setup quality
2. **Confirmation must be super strong** — wait for significant move before adding
3. **Experience required** — not for beginners; requires skill and confidence

### Fundamental Rule
**You can pyramid ONLY if you are willing to:**
- Raise your RPT (risk per trade), OR
- Raise your stop-loss, OR
- Do both

### Location Rules
| Rule | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Within the base** | Pyramid only while stock is still in the base structure |
| **Not substantially above breakout** | Do not pyramid far above the breakout zone |
| **After partial exit** | Can pyramid even after taking partial profits, as long as still within base |
| **Must be a setup** | Each pyramid entry must qualify as an independent setup (pullback + contraction + trigger) |

### Pyramiding Adjustments

#### Stop-Loss Options
1. **Keep same** (conservative) — increases position size moderately
2. **Raise stop-loss** (moderate) — increases position size more significantly

#### RPT Options
1. **Make it zero** — if stop raised enough, can add shares without increasing total risk
2. **Keep same** (e.g., 0.5% → 0.5%) — moderate approach
3. **Raise RPT** (e.g., 0.5% → 0.8% or 1%) — aggressive approach

### Pyramiding Scenarios

#### Scenario A: Pyramiding Near Entry Price
- **Cannot raise stop-loss** (price hasn't moved enough)
- **Must raise RPT** to pyramid

#### Scenario B: Pyramiding at Higher Price
- **Can raise stop-loss** (sufficient distance from entry)
- **Can raise RPT**
- **Can do both** (all combinations available)

### Pyramid Calculator Usage
**Inputs:**
- Account value
- Initial entry price & stop-loss
- Initial quantity & RPT
- New entry price (pyramid)
- Revised stop-loss
- Revised RPT (0%, same, or higher)

**Outputs:**
- Quantity to buy for pyramid
- Total position size
- Combined average price
- Total risk if stop hit
- Risk-reward ratio

### Mathematical Impact Example
**Trader A (No Pyramiding):**
- Capital: 5M, RPT: 0.5%, RR: 3, Win rate: 40%, 40 trades/quarter
- Annual return: **57%**

**Trader B (5 Pyramided Trades/Quarter):**
- Same base metrics
- 5 trades at 1% RPT with 60% win rate
- Annual return: **70%**

**Trader B (Aggressive — 4/5 pyramids win):**
- 5 trades at 1% RPT with 70% win rate
- Annual return: **90%**

### Key Warnings
- **Do not force pyramids** — only when chart makes it obvious
- **Do not pyramid randomly** — must be at proper setup locations
- **Do not pyramid out of the base** — only within or near breakout
- **Wait for strong confirmation** — gap-ups at right location are ideal
- **Pay higher price for confirmation** — better to be right than early

## Live Entries (15-Minute Chart)

### Definition
Entering 100% of position using intraday timing (vs. 50% live + 50% close entry).

### Why Live Entries Are Better
1. **Lower cost price** — enter earlier in the move
2. **Tighter stop-losses** — use 15-min structure for precision stops
3. **Larger position size** — tighter stops allow more shares
4. **Enhanced risk-reward** — combination of above factors

### Why Live Entries Are Harder
1. **Better timing required** — must identify exact entry points
2. **Quick decision-making** — based on lower timeframe action
3. **Alert throughout day** — higher time commitment
4. **More effort** — frequent chart monitoring during market hours

### Using 15-Minute Chart

#### Process
1. Identify setup on daily chart
2. Switch to 15-minute chart
3. Look for **clean structures** and **resistance levels**
4. Wait for **volume expansion** and **breakout confirmation**
5. Enter on breakout with strong volume

#### Stop-Loss Rules
- **Use 15-min to tighten stops, not widen them**
- Identify swing lows on 15-min chart
- Ensure stop-loss ≤ daily TRP (do not exceed it)

#### Confirmation Signals
- **Gap-ups** at right location (strong confirmation)
- **Back-to-back solid green candles** with volume
- **Clear breakout** of 15-min resistance with volume spike
- **Multiple attempts** at resistance followed by breakout

### Discretionary Live Entry Examples

#### Example 1: No Proper Contraction on Daily
- Stock pulls back but doesn't form clean contraction
- 15-min chart shows **significant volume expansion** + **clean breakout structure**
- Can enter on 15-min confirmation even without daily contraction

#### Example 2: Pre-Breakout Entry
- Daily trigger level not yet broken
- 15-min shows: volume spike → pullback → contraction → strong green candle
- Can enter **before daily breakout** if 15-min structure is perfect

#### Example 3: Earnings Flush + Gap-Up
- Stock flushes on earnings, then gaps up
- Wait for gap to sustain and stock to move higher (don't rush)
- Enter as stock confirms strength (pay higher for confirmation)

## Margin Trading / Leverage

### Purpose
In **strong bull markets**, to maximize opportunities when:
- Total open risk is zero or near-zero (all trades trailed to cost)
- Exposure is 100% (fully invested)
- New setups keep appearing

### Methods to Get Leverage (India)

#### 1. Futures
- **Automatic leverage**: 4-5x (₹6-8L position with ₹1-2L margin)
- **Cost benefit**: Very low STT (Securities Transaction Tax)
- **Preference**: Choose futures over stocks when both available

#### 2. MTF (Margin Trading Facility)
- **Loan from broker**: e.g., 70% funded by broker, 30% by trader
- **Not all stocks covered**: Broker-specific list
- **Limits vary by stock**: More liquid/less volatile = higher limit
- **Total loan limit**: ₹35-60L (broker-dependent)
- **Interest**: ~0.045% per day on loan amount

#### 3. Pledging (Collateral Margin)
- **Pledge existing stocks** → get margin to buy more
- **50/50 rule**: Max 50% of new purchase can be collateral (rest must be cash)
- **Interest**: 0.048% per day on amount exceeding 50% rule
- **Instant**: Pledge/unpledge instantly (broker-dependent)

### Leverage Strategy in Bull Markets
1. Take trades with standard RPT
2. As trades hit mathematical targets → raise stops to cost (risk-free)
3. Open risk decreases → space for new trades
4. Take more trades → repeat process
5. End up with many risk-free positions + 100% exposure
6. Use leverage to take new trades beyond account value
7. Exposure can go up to **400%** while open risk stays within limits

## Discretionary Stop-Loss Adjustments

### Standard Methods (Review)
1. **Standard stop**: Entry price - TRP
2. **Conservative stop**: Trigger level - TRP (broader)
3. **Aggressive stop**: Low of entry candle OR swing low (tighter)

### Combination Approach
- **50% at aggressive stop** (e.g., swing low)
- **50% at standard stop** (entry - TRP)

### Discretionary Adjustments

#### Exit Before Stop in Weak Market
- **Scenario**: Weak market conditions, stock returns to cost price
- **Action**: Exit at cost without waiting for stop-loss
- **Reason**: Pilot trade in weak market — preserve capital

#### Full Exit on Extension (Weak Market)
- **Scenario**: Low RPT trade (0.1-0.3%) makes a move
- **Action**: Exit 100% on first extension (not 20-30%)
- **Reason**: Position too small to manage; clear portfolio

#### Partial Exit on Extreme Extension
- **Scenario**: Stock extremely extended, already exited 60%
- **Action**: Exit only 20% (not 40%), hold final 20% with tight trailing stop
- **Reason**: Analysis suggests more upside possible

#### Exit at Cost vs. Wait for Stop
- **Tight stop**: Exit at cost if stock returns to entry (stop too close)
- **Broad stop**: Can hold below cost, wait for actual stop-loss

## Discretion: Core Principles

### What Discretion IS
- **Thoughtful adjustments** in certain situations
- **Full awareness** of context and implications
- **Careful decisions** based on experience and intuition

### What Discretion IS NOT
| Not Discretion | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| Holding past stop-loss because "stock will recover" | Breaking primary rule |
| Lowering stop-loss as stock approaches it | Breaking primary rule |
| Oversizing after losses to "recover" | Emotional trading |
| Buying strong stock that isn't a setup | Breaking setup rules |

### Building Intuition for Discretion
1. **Consistently follow system** over long period
2. **Dedication and passion** for markets
3. **Deep understanding** of market dynamics
4. **Experience** — only for sufficiently experienced traders

### When to Apply Discretion
- **Beginners**: Do NOT use discretion — follow strict rules
- **Experienced traders**: Can enhance performance significantly when applied correctly
