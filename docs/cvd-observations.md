## Core Principle: No Tell is a Tell

**"CVD has no tell" is itself a signal — not an absence of information.**

When CVD shows no pre-run accumulation pattern before a price move:
- The move is more likely event-driven, catalyst-driven, or momentum-chasing
- There is no evidence of informed buyers pre-positioning
- The move is more likely to be short-lived, sharper, or reverse faster
- No tell = reactive money, not proactive money

**The taxonomy:**

| CVD state | What it means | How to trade |
|---|---|---|
| Clear tell (absorption, zero-wick build) | Informed buyers loaded before price moved | Structure supports the move; follow with confidence |
| Ambiguous / mixed | Contested — both sides active | Wait for clarity; don't force a read |
| No tell | Reactive/event move — no pre-positioning | Be cautious about chasing; move may lack follow-through |
| Wick-dominant at highs | Distribution — sellers absorbing demand | Active warning; prior tells may be failing |

**Never force CVD to say something it isn't saying.** Absence of a pattern is a pattern. Reading "no tell" correctly is just as valuable as reading a tell — it keeps you out of reactive moves dressed up as setups.

## Price-CVD Correlation and the Two-Regime Question — Session 2026-08-15

> **CAUTION: Small sample sizes (n=41–43 per regime across 8 stocks). These are observations to investigate further, not rules.**

### The hypothesis going in

Absorption tell (tail>>wick, high volume) should only be valid when:
1. Price is falling or flat (not already running)
2. CVD is low in the anchor window (not at peak)

The theory: same candle at the bottom = accumulation. Same candle at the top = distribution. Context determines meaning.

### What the data actually shows

Tested 84 absorption sessions (tail>>wick 5:1+, vol 2x+ average) across 8 stocks.

**Two regimes:**

| Regime | Definition | n | RUN % | FAIL % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Momentum** | Price up >3% AND CVD in top 50% | 43 | 49% | 28% |
| **Reversal** | Price flat/down OR CVD low | 41 | 24% | 22% |

**The hypothesis was wrong in an interesting way.**

The momentum regime (absorption during a run, CVD high) has a *higher* hit rate (49%) than the reversal regime (24%). But it also has a higher fail rate (28% vs 22%).

The reversal regime has a lower hit rate but also lower failure — more outcomes land in FLAT (price +2% to +5% over 20 days), which isn't captured as a RUN.

### Why the hypothesis was partly wrong

Looking at the momentum RUN cases: LLOYDSENGG Jun 2025, NETWEB Sep 2025, FCL May 2026 — these are all absorption sessions *within an ongoing strong run*. The absorption isn't reversing — it's confirming continued demand during pullbacks inside a trend. The buyers showing up are real. The run continues.

So the absorption tell has **two valid uses**, not one:

1. **Reversal signal** — absorption at price low, CVD low in window. Predicts a new run from a base. Hit rate ~24% by 20-day window but lower false positives.

2. **Continuation signal** — absorption during a pullback inside an existing run, CVD high but price momentarily weak. Hit rate ~49%. Higher risk but also higher frequency of RUN outcomes.

**The false positives in the original analysis were specifically:** absorption at price *highs* (not pullbacks) with CVD at peaks. That's when someone is distributing into momentum. That case sits between the two regimes — not a clean pullback, not a base reversal.

### Stock-level differences matter

| Stock | Best regime | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| LLOYDSENGG | Momentum (67% RUN) | Strong trend stock — continuation signals work |
| NETWEB | Momentum (80% RUN) | Same — momentum continuation very reliable here |
| FCL | Momentum (75% RUN) | Event-driven but continuation works |
| ABB | Reversal (44% RUN, 11% FAIL) | Best reversal profile — low failure |
| MARUTI | Neither | Low hit in both, too many FLAT outcomes |
| 3MINDIA | Neither | Thin stock — absorption signals weak overall |
| DREDGECORP | Neither | Event-driven, CVD signals unreliable |

### What this means practically

The simple "absorption at low CVD = good, absorption at high CVD = bad" rule does NOT hold consistently across the data.

A more nuanced framework:

- **For trend/momentum stocks (LLOYDSENGG, NETWEB, FCL):** absorption sessions during pullbacks within a run are valid continuation signals even when CVD is high.
- **For base-building stocks (ABB):** reversal signals from low CVD with price in decline have lower hit rate but fewer false positives.
- **For thin/event-driven stocks (DREDGECORP, GALAXYSURF, 3MINDIA):** CVD absorption signals are unreliable in both regimes.

### The price-CVD divergence as originally proposed

Tested strictly (price falling + CVD rising simultaneously): only 11 cases, 0% RUN rate, 36% FAIL. Too restrictive — the combination almost never occurs, and when it does, it doesn't predict runs.

The more useful divergence is **sequence-based**: price made a lower low, CVD made a higher low over the same window. That's what we found qualitatively in SBIN (Apr 7 2025), MARUTI (Nov 2024), LOYDSENGG (Dec 2024). The point-in-time slope filter missed this.

### Working conclusion (with caution)

> **The absorption tell has two regimes with different implications:**
> 1. Continuation within trend: works best for high-momentum stocks with persistent CVD builds
> 2. Reversal from base: lower hit rate but fewer false positives, works best for mid-cap quality stocks
>
> The original "CVD low = signal" rule was oversimplified. The actual filter is **regime context** — is this stock in an established trend or base-building?
>
> Sample sizes are small (8 stocks, 84 total sessions). This needs more data before treating as a rule. Treat as a working hypothesis to test on new stocks as they are analysed.



> When the absorption tell appeared but the run did NOT follow.

**16 cases found across LLOYDSENGG, NETWEB, FCL, ABB, MARUTI.**

### Root cause: 12 of 16 were tells at the TOP, not the bottom

The absorption signature (tail>>wick on high volume) has opposite meanings depending on WHERE it appears in the CVD cycle:

- **CVD low/negative + price falling → buyers absorbing supply = ACCUMULATION** → run follows
- **CVD high/at peak + price at high → buyers chasing, sellers distributing** → failure

Same candle shape. Completely different context. Context is everything.

### Two filters that remove 14 of 16 false positives

**Filter 1 — CVD position:** Only treat absorption as accumulation when CVD is in the lower half of the anchor window range or crossing from negative to positive. Absorption at CVD peaks = distribution.

**Filter 2 — Price position:** Tell is more reliable when price is in a base or downtrend. Absorption at price highs = buyers being trapped, not buying at value.

### The 2 remaining (noise / macro override)
- ABB Feb 4 2025: zero-wick after anchor reset but CVD still negative. Premature — needs CVD to actually cross positive first.
- MARUTI Feb 1 2025: genuine accumulation signal, overridden by a broader market selloff. CVD tell was real but macro was stronger.

### Updated working rule
> Absorption tell (tail>>wick, high volume) is signal only when:
> 1. CVD is LOW in the anchor window (not at a peak), AND
> 2. Price is in a base or decline (not at highs)
>
> The same candle at a CVD peak with price at highs = distribution, not accumulation. Treat as the opposite signal.

## Session: 2026-08-15

### Methodology developed

- CVD anchor resets to zero at the start of each anchor period. Values are only comparable within the same window — never across a reset.
- Shorter anchor (3M) gives earlier signals than longer anchor (6M/12M). A stock can look bearish on 6M CVD while 3M CVD is already positive — that divergence is worth noting.
- CVD should be read as OHLC, not just close. `tail` (close−low) = intrabar buyer recovery. `wick` (high−close) = intrabar demand failure. `tail >> wick` on high volume = buyers in control. `wick >> tail` = sellers absorbed the demand.
- The working question for any stock: was there a CVD tell before any prior significant move? Is anything similar happening now?
